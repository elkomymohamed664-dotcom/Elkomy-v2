Pharmacokinetics & Molecular Antiemetic
Mechanisms
CONTRAINDICATIONS, ADVERSE EFFECTS &
INTERACTIONS
Kinetics of [6]-Gingerol: Rapid oral absorption (Ka ≈ 12.2 h−1;
plasma Cmax in 10–45 min, 2-compartment model); extensive
hepatic/intestinal Phase II glucuronidation & sulfation (free
parent clears within ~2 h).
▪
Receptor Targets: Non-competitive antagonism of human
intestinal/vagal 5-HT₃ receptors (Walstab et al., 2013) + NK-1
(substance P) modulation + weak muscarinic antagonism.
▪
Gastric Rhythm & Vasopressin: Suppresses circular vection-
induced tachygastria and blunts plasma vasopressin
elevation (~21%), prolonging nausea latency (Lien et al., 2003).
▪
Clinical Scope: Open-sea RCT (Grøntved, 1988) showed 72%
vomiting protection & ↓ cold sweats, whereas rotating-chair
testing (Stewart, 1991) showed central vestibular protection
requires anticholinergic agents (e.g., scopolamine).
▪
Coagulopathy & Surgery: Inhibits thromboxane B₂ (TXB₂) &
platelet aggregation. Contraindicated in bleeding disorders;
discontinue concentrated supplements ≥ 2 weeks pre-op
(MSKCC) and avoid post-op.
▪
Biliary & Pregnancy Limits: Cholagogic stimulation
contraindicates therapeutic doses in cholelithiasis; medicinal
extracts in pregnancy/lactation require medical oversight.
▪
Adverse Effects: Epigastric heartburn, mild diarrhea,
oral/pharyngeal mucosal irritation; rare Zingiberaceae
hypersensitivity (no direct cross-reactivity with pollen or
peanut allergens).
▪
Drug–Herb Interactions: Warfarin, Dabigatran, Aspirin,
Clopidogrel (case reports of severe GI hemorrhage), NSAIDs
(additive bleeding/ulcer risk), and antidiabetics (additive
hypoglycemia).
▪

CELLULAR PHARMACOLOGY & ROUTE-DEPENDENT
KINETICS
TOXICOLOGY, CONTRAINDICATIONS & SPECIAL
POPULATIONS
CRITICAL PEDIATRIC HAZARD (< 2 Years — EMA /
NCCIH)
Applying menthol/peppermint oil near an infant's face or
nares stimulates laryngeal TRPM8 receptors, provoking the
Kratschmer reflex—sudden laryngospasm,
bronchospasm & fatal reflex apnea (plus
hemolysis/jaundice in G6PD deficiency). Oral capsules are
contraindicated < 8 yrs.
Ca²⁺ Channel Spasmolysis: Displaces [³H]-nitrendipine at L-
type Ca²⁺ channels in GI smooth muscle, blocking Ca²⁺ influx
(basis for ACG IBS guidelines).
▪
Inhaled Antiemesis: Olfactory/trigeminal TRPM8 signaling
rapidly attenuates nausea (2024/2025 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs
across post-op, CINV, and pregnancy nausea).
▪
Enteric Capsules vs. Antacids / PPIs / H₂ Blockers: Acid
suppression elevates gastric pH (> 5.5), causing premature
enteric-coating dissolution → intragastric menthol release →
LES relaxation & acute heartburn.
▪
Inhalation in GERD / Hiatal Hernia: Bypasses gastric PPI
interaction, yet mucosal/swallowed menthol can still relax a
weakened LES or irritate mucosa—use cautiously.
▪
CYP3A4 Inhibition: 600 mg oral oil reversibly inhibits CYP3A4,
increasing plasma AUC/Cmax of felodipine & cyclosporine
(EMA).
▪
Geriatric/Adult Limits: Contraindicated in menthol allergy,
severe GERD / hiatal hernia (oral), and cholelithiasis /
cholangitis (choleretic action).
▪
Pregnancy & Lactation: Medicinal oral oil/leaf not
recommended (EMA). Topical nipple gels require wiping clean
pre-nursing; maternal inhalation must stay strictly away from
the infant.
▪
Acute Menthol Toxicity (Est. lethal dose 50–150 mg/kg):
Epigastric burns, ataxia, confusion, cardiac arrhythmias,
seizures, renal/respiratory failure, and coma; triggers
bronchospasm in reactive-airway/asthma patients.
▪

SOLANACEAE • NARROW THERAPEUTIC INDEX ALKALOID SOURCE
Black Henbane — Hyoscyamus
niger L.
Dried Leaves (0.045–0.14%) & Seeds (0.5–0.6%) • Standardized Active
Drugs: Hyoscyamine & Scopolamine
PART USED
Leaves &
Seeds
MARKERS
Tropane
Alkaloids
TARGET
CNS M₁–M₅
Microscopic & Chemical Identity
Glandular-pubescent leaves with anisocytic
stomata and Ca-oxalate prism/cluster crystals;
pitted reniform seeds. (–)-Hyoscyamine racemizes
to (±)-atropine; Scopolamine bears a 6,7-β-epoxide.
CNS Pharmacodynamics & Crude Toxicity
6,7-epoxide lowers pKa (~7.75 vs. atropine 9.8) →
rapid BBB entry & vestibular M₁/M₃ blockade (60–
80% efficacy). Crude plant is toxic/obsolete (lethal
scopolamine ~2–4 mg; ~4 flowers poison a child).

OFFICIAL PHARMACOPOEIAL MONOGRAPHS & PEER-REVIEWED REFERENCES
Therapeutic vs. Toxic Signs: Xerostomia, mydriasis/cycloplegia,
tachycardia, constipation, retention, sedation → overdose triggers
delirium, hallucinations, hyperthermia, anhidrosis, ileus, seizures
(Antidote: Physostigmine).
▪
Absolute Contraindications: Narrow-angle glaucoma (IOP spike),
BPH/urinary retention, GI obstruction, myasthenia gravis,
tachyarrhythmias/CVD, pregnancy & lactation, peds & elderly,
and Down syndrome.
▪
Anticholinergics & 1st-Gen Antihistamines: Synergistic dry
mouth, blurred vision, ileus, urinary retention, sedation & CNS
delirium.
▪
TCAs & Low-Potency Antipsychotics: Potent additive
anticholinergic, hyperthermic & cardiac conduction toxicity.
▪
Sympathomimetics & Driving: Additive tachycardia risk; strictly
avoid driving or machinery (cycloplegia + sedation).
▪
